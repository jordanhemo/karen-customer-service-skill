# Karen playbook

Read this when selecting a tone, channel, or escalation step.

## Tone dial

### Polite

Use for the first contact or an obvious mistake.

> I’m contacting you to request [remedy] for [issue]. On [date], [brief fact]. Please confirm [action] by [date].

### Firm

Use after a failed answer, broken promise, or meaningful delay.

> I contacted you on [dates] and was told [promise]. That has not happened. I’m requesting [remedy] now. Please confirm in writing by [date] or route this to someone authorized to resolve it.

### Final notice

Use after documented attempts have failed and a real next channel exists.

> This is my final attempt to resolve the matter directly. If I do not receive confirmation of [remedy] by [date], I will consider [truthful, proportionate next step].

Only name an escalation the user can legitimately take.

## Remedy menu

Prefer a precise remedy over “make this right”:

- full or partial refund with amount and payment method
- replacement with shipping deadline
- fee reversal or account credit
- cancellation without penalty and written confirmation
- repair under warranty
- correction of an account, invoice, or service record
- delivery or completion by a stated date

## Escalation ladder

Use the lowest reasonable rung and advance one step at a time:

1. normal support channel
2. written follow-up quoting the case number
3. supervisor or specialist team
4. formal complaints or executive resolution channel
5. payment dispute, marketplace claim, ombudsman, or regulator when applicable
6. qualified legal advice or court process when proportionate

Do not recommend a payment dispute before checking timing, evidence, merchant-contact requirements, and the effect on any ongoing service.

## Evidence packet

Ask for only what helps:

- receipt, invoice, or order confirmation
- relevant policy captured with date and URL
- screenshots or photos
- delivery tracking or service log
- support transcript
- names, dates, case numbers, and promised actions

Redact account credentials, full payment details, and unrelated personal data.

## Phone script

“Hi, I’m calling about [reference]. The issue is [one sentence]. I’ve already [prior attempt]. I’m asking for [specific remedy]. Are you authorized to do that? If not, please transfer me to someone who is. Before we finish, may I have the case number and your confirmation of the next step?”

## Refusal response

Do not argue in circles. Ask:

1. “What exact policy prevents this remedy?”
2. “Where can I read that policy?”
3. “Who is authorized to review an exception?”
4. “Please note in the case that I requested [remedy] and it was declined.”
